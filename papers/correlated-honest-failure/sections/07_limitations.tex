\section{Limitations and claim boundaries}

This paper combines one direct protocol-rule derivation with several declared model extensions. The distinction is essential.

\paragraph{Correlated fault domains are hypothetical inputs, not observed JAM failures.}
The paper does not establish that a currently deployed or planned JAM client contains a fault shared by any particular validator fraction. The fault-domain model asks what follows if such a dependency exists.

\paragraph{The ELVES branching substitution is an extension.}
Replacing the original independently honest corrective share by the fault-specific independently correct share $h_x$ is analytically transparent, but it is outside the assumptions under which the published ELVES result was derived. Its interpretation therefore requires review by the ELVES authors.

\paragraph{The pessimistic branching process is not an exact protocol simulator.}
The reproduction number and extinction expressions are inherited from a branching approximation. They identify structural thresholds under that approximation; they do not constitute an exact JAM attack probability.

\paragraph{Fault domains are not equivalent to client labels.}
Two validators running the same nominal client may differ in version, dependencies, configuration, hardware, or execution environment. Conversely, nominally different clients may share libraries, compilers, specifications, or conceptual defects. The relevant object is fault-specific correlated response, not a client-name count.

\paragraph{The slow maintenance state is declared rather than protocol-native.}
The variables $C_t$, $O_t$, and $R_t$ and coefficients $r_C,r_O,r_R,k_{CO},k_{OR},k_{RC}$ are analytical state variables introduced to expose a cross-audit maintenance question. They are not claimed to be Gray Paper state variables, and the paper does not currently estimate them from a JAM deployment or testnet.

\paragraph{The recurrent product threshold is not claimed as new mathematics.}
The three-process replacement inequality is a finite positive-systems/reproduction-number structure. The contribution claimed here is its explicit separation from the event-level ELVES correction condition and the resulting JAM-facing systems question, not the product threshold itself.

\paragraph{The dynamic return theorem is local.}
The equivalence
\[
\lambda_{t+1}\ge1
\iff
Fk_{RC}R_t\ge1-r_C
\]
is proved at current criticality under the declared one-step state update. It is not a general stability theorem for the full three-dimensional maintenance system, nor does it establish that the system converges to a particular equilibrium.

\paragraph{The reduced multiplier is diagnostic, not calibrated.}
The reduced path $\lambda_t=m^t\lambda_0$ is used to prove that current reproduction alone does not identify finite-horizon resilience. The witness $m=9/10$ is deliberately transparent and is not an estimate of JAM attrition, client turnover, or any protocol parameter. A deployment forecast would require empirical identification of the slow dynamics and uncertainty around them.

\paragraph{Current supercriticality is not equivalent to guaranteed audit success.}
The condition $\lambda_x>1$ is a supercritical branching condition in the declared ELVES extension. The paper does not equate it with certainty of correct resolution for a particular audit, and the dynamic sections therefore discuss preservation of the reproduction condition rather than guaranteed outcomes.

\paragraph{Client-diversity incentives do not automatically close the maintenance loop.}
Existing work shows that client identity can in principle be made verifiable and minority implementations can be differentially rewarded. Such mechanisms may instantiate part of the observability/resource-return pathway, but this paper does not assume that they are sufficient to maintain fault-specific independent corrective capacity. That sufficiency is itself an empirical systems question.

\paragraph{Protocol and ecosystem boundaries remain partly delegated.}
Operator economics, staking consequences, implementation development, software maintenance and deployment choices span layers beyond execution auditing. The model deliberately exposes this boundary rather than attributing every slow-loop coefficient to the JAM protocol itself.

These limitations are not secondary caveats. They define the intended claim hierarchy: direct Gray Paper verdict arithmetic first; ELVES-based fault-domain extension second; recurrent-maintenance and dynamic-resilience models third; empirical grounding and protocol design conclusions only after those mappings survive expert review and measurement.
